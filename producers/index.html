The trucking window

A new trucking company applies to the federal government for operating authority.

The authority stays pending until an insurance company files proof of coverage.

Until that filing lands, the company cannot legally start. The application is dated, and the wait is the opening.

We watch the federal trucking files and pull the businesses that applied, are still waiting, and have no filing on file yet.

  • 9,239 businesses applied for federal trucking authority from September 22 to October 22, 2026.
  • 6,479 were still waiting on October 22, with no insurance filing on file. That is about 1,450 new waiting businesses a week.
  • 6,403 more were granted authority in the same month. A granted carrier has a filing on file, so the granted list is the second call: new authority, first real policy, first renewal ahead.
  • Most rows are trucking companies that haul goods. Freight brokers, household goods movers, and passenger carriers sit in the same files, in smaller numbers.

Why call a carrier that already filed?

A filing is a coverage amount, not a finished policy. Most granted carriers filed at $750,000 or $1,000,000, the amounts the federal rules ask for.

A new authority still has to place its real policy, set its limits, and face its first renewal. The waiting list is the first call, and the granted list is the second one; both lists carry the dates that matter.

Commercial openings

A business that pulls a permit is building a location, expanding, or changing what it does on site. Coverage changes at the permit, and again at the opening. There is no national permit file, so this report is built city by city and county by county, and it prints what it covers.

Two tiers. Tier 1 is the buying window: a permit is issued or work has started, which is when landlords, lenders, and contractors ask for certificates. Tier 2 is early awareness: projects in planning, months out, so you hear about them before a sign goes up.

Storm canvass lists

After a hail or wind storm, the roofs that got fixed leave a permit. The ones that did not are a list a roofer can walk.

We build the list from the federal storm record and the city's permit file: every address near a recorded storm point, minus every address that pulled a roof permit after the storm. What is left is not a damage claim. It is where to look first.

San Antonio, Texas — where the test covers houses

San Antonio requires a permit to repair or replace a roof — the city's own homeowner page says so, with no exceptions for ordinary re-roofs. A permitted re-roof leaves a record, so an address still on this list is an address where damaging weather was recorded nearby and no re-roof permit followed.

  • 54 qualifying storms crossed Bexar County from January 2024 through September 2026: 52 hail events at 1 inch or larger — the largest was 3 inches — and 2 damaging wind events.
  • 15,787 re-roof permits in the city's files for the same window, each one typed as a re-roof permit by the city.
  • Of 16,644 addresses within 1.5 km of a storm point, 3,703 pulled a re-roof permit after their storm and were crossed off.
  • The list: 12,941 addresses on 2,588 streets. The fresh part — 4,377 addresses near the 2025 and 2026 storms — is worked first.

Austin, Texas — commercial weight, stated plainly

  • 45 qualifying storms in Travis County, January 2024 through September 2026: 42 hail, 2 damaging wind, and 1 tornado that did $5.00M in damage at Manor.
  • The Austin list: 11,843 addresses on 1,939 streets.
  • Austin lets most homeowners replace a roof covering without a permit. For houses, this list is a starting map, not a record test. The absence test carries its weight on commercial buildings, structural work, and the wildland-urban interface, where Austin does require permits.

A storm point is where the weather service recorded the storm — a spotter report or a survey point — not a line around every damaged roof. No permit never proves no work was done: work done without a permit leaves no record. The list decides where to look; the roof is judged from the street.

The roof line

Insurers price roofs by age, and older roofs lose coverage options first. Nobody can see a roof's age from the street — but a permitted re-roof leaves a dated record.

More windows, built territory by territory

Trucking is federal, so one file covers the whole country. The windows below are kept by states, counties, and cities. We build them one territory at a time, and we say plainly what each territory covers.

Health permits and new restaurants

A new restaurant cannot open without a health permit and an opening inspection.

Health departments keep those records. A first inspection usually means an opening is close, and an opening means a new policy or a changed one.

New business filings

Every new LLC and corporation files with its Secretary of State. The filing is the earliest public record a business makes.

Some states sell the full list as a data file. Where they do, new filings can be cut by week and by county, and a new business at a commercial address is a business about to sign a lease, a loan, or a contract that asks for insurance.

Coverage is set for your territory before you enroll. The report prints what it covers, and what it does not. Row counts differ by territory: big states run into the hundreds each month, and small territories run a handful.
